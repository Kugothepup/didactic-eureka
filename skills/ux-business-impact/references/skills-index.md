# The 99 UX Skills

The full taxonomy, organised into four stages. Each stage has a divergent phase (expanding) and a convergent phase (contracting), plus a continuous track that runs alongside everything.

Use this file to go in either direction: from a skill to its likely impacts, or from a needed impact back to the skills that produce it.

**Never invent a skill code or rename one.** If work doesn't map to anything here, say so plainly.

## Conditions for success

A skill does not produce an impact on expertise alone. Before promising an impact, check three conditions:

- **Scope** — does the work reach far enough to move the metric? A content audit of one page will not reduce maintenance cost.
- **Authority** — can the person act on the findings, or only report them? An audit nobody is empowered to act on produces no impact.
- **Resources** — is there budget, time and engineering capacity to implement?

Naming a blocked condition early is more useful than delivering a skill that cannot land. When a condition is missing, the honest output is the blocker, not the artefact.

## A note on automation

Skills requiring deep expertise, collaborative problem-solving, and synthesis of real-world observation are the hardest to automate and carry the most durable career value — building trust, aligning teams, and interpreting complex human need in context. Weight investment toward those when advising on skill development.
